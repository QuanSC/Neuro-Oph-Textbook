Traumatic optic neuropathy

Mechanisms
Direct: penetrating injury, bone fragment or hematoma compressing or transecting the nerve; optic nerve avulsion. Indirect: force transmitted to the nerve, most often in the optic canal where it's tethered to bone, typically after a blow to the brow.
Diagnosis
Reduced acuity, color and field with an RAPD after trauma; the disc usually looks normal at first and turns pale over weeks. CT with thin cuts through the optic canal looks for fractures and fragments.
What the evidence says
The International Optic Nerve Trauma Study (nonrandomized, 133 patients) found no clear benefit of corticosteroids or optic canal decompression over observation: 3 or more lines of improvement occurred in 57% untreated, 52% with steroids and 32% with surgery, after adjusting for baseline acuity. The CRASH trial of high-dose methylprednisolone in significant head injury found higher mortality with steroids (21% vs 18%).
Practical bottom line
Neither steroids nor canal surgery is standard of care for indirect TON; observation is reasonable, and high-dose steroids should be avoided in patients with significant head injury. Surgery may be considered for a compressive bone fragment or hematoma, individualized with the trauma and skull base teams.

Where the numbers come from

  • IONTS: 133 patients with indirect TON (nonrandomized); no significant difference in final acuity between observation, steroids and optic canal decompression after adjusting for baseline; neither treatment considered standard of care: Levin LA, Beck RW, Joseph MP, Seiff S, Kraker R. The treatment of traumatic optic neuropathy: the International Optic Nerve Trauma Study. Ophthalmology. 1999;106(7):1268-1277. doi:10.1016/S0161-6420(99)00707-1. Improvement rates (57%, 52%, 32%) as summarized in Yu-Wai-Man P, Griffiths PG. Steroids for traumatic optic neuropathy. Cochrane Database Syst Rev. 2013;(6):CD006032.
  • CRASH: high-dose methylprednisolone within 8 hours of significant head injury (10,008 adults) increased 2-week mortality (21% vs 18%): CRASH trial collaborators. Effect of intravenous corticosteroids on death within 14 days in 10008 adults with clinically significant head injury (MRC CRASH trial). Lancet. 2004;364(9442):1321-1328.
  • Fracture repair timing and retrobulbar hemorrhage management reflect standard teaching; follow institutional trauma and oculoplastic protocols.
