Watch the eyes, read the trace

Pick a type. The eyes move as you face the patient; the trace below plots eye position over time, the way an eye-movement recording would. Up on the trace means rightward (or upward, or clockwise for torsion, as labeled).

Peripheral or central?

FeaturePeripheral vestibularCentral
DirectionUnidirectional, mixed horizontal-torsional, beats away from the lesionCan be purely vertical or purely torsional, or change direction with gaze
FixationSuppressed by fixation (worse with Frenzel lenses or penlight-cover test)Little or no suppression
Alexander lawStronger when looking toward the fast phaseVariable
Head impulseAbnormal (corrective saccade)Usually normal
SkewAbsent or smallOften present
Other signsHearing loss or tinnitus may be present (also consider AICA stroke)Ataxia, dysarthria, other brainstem or cerebellar signs

HINTS: in acute continuous vertigo with nystagmus, a normal head impulse, direction-changing nystagmus or a skew points to stroke. In high-risk patients, any one dangerous sign was 100% sensitive and 96% specific for stroke, outperforming early MRI (Kattah 2009). Add new hearing loss ("HINTS plus") because AICA strokes can mimic peripheral disease.

Treatment by type

Evidence note: few randomized trials exist, and most drug use for nystagmus is off-label. The trials that do exist are cited in the sources tab.

Where the numbers come from

  • Memantine and gabapentin improved visual acuity, nystagmus intensity and foveation in congenital (infantile) nystagmus in a randomized, double-masked, placebo-controlled trial (48 patients, 56 days): McLean R, Proudlock F, Thomas S, Degg C, Gottlob I. Congenital nystagmus: randomized, controlled, double-masked trial of memantine/gabapentin. Ann Neurol. 2007;61(2):130-138. doi:10.1002/ana.21065.
  • Gabapentin and memantine for acquired nystagmus: Thurtell MJ, Joshi AC, Leone AC, et al. Crossover trial of gabapentin and memantine as treatment for acquired nystagmus. Ann Neurol. 2010;67(5):676-680. Starck M, Albrecht H, Pollmann W, Dieterich M, Straube A. Acquired pendular nystagmus in multiple sclerosis: an examiner-blind cross-over treatment study of memantine and gabapentin. J Neurol. 2010;257(3):322-327.
  • Aminopyridines for downbeat nystagmus: Strupp M, Schüler O, Krafczyk S, et al. Treatment of downbeat nystagmus with 3,4-diaminopyridine: a placebo-controlled study. Neurology. 2003;61(2):165-170.
  • HINTS performance in acute vestibular syndrome: Kattah JC, Talkad AV, Wang DZ, Hsieh YH, Newman-Toker DE. Stroke. 2009;40(11):3504-3510.
  • Waveforms are schematic and time is compressed for periodic alternating nystagmus. Other content reflects standard teaching in the core texts.
