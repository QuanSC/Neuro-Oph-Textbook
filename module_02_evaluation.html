Where the numbers come from

  • HINTS 100% sensitive and 96% specific for stroke; early DWI falsely negative in 12% (all within 48 hours): Kattah JC, Talkad AV, Wang DZ, Hsieh YH, Newman-Toker DE. HINTS to diagnose stroke in the acute vestibular syndrome. Stroke. 2009;40(11):3504-3510. doi:10.1161/STROKEAHA.109.551234. Note: high-risk patients with acute vestibular syndrome, examined by trained clinicians.
  • About 19% of normal people show anisocoria of 0.4 mm or more at any given exam: Lam BL, Thompson HS, Corbett JJ. The prevalence of simple anisocoria. Am J Ophthalmol. 1987;104(1):69-73. doi:10.1016/0002-9394(87)90296-0.
  • Upright-supine test, a drop of 50% or more favors skew: Wong AMF. Understanding skew deviation and a new clinical test to differentiate it from trochlear nerve palsy. J AAPOS. 2010;14(1):61-67.
  • Cirrus and Spectralis RNFL values are not interchangeable (normal eyes, mean average RNFL 98.7 microns Cirrus vs 106.6 microns Spectralis): Seibold LK, Mandava N, Kahook MY. Comparison of retinal nerve fiber layer thickness in normal eyes using time-domain and spectral-domain optical coherence tomography. Am J Ophthalmol. 2010;150(6):807-814. doi:10.1016/j.ajo.2010.06.024.
  • The between-device gap comes mainly from scan path and segmentation (instrument-derived global difference 6.7 microns, reduced to 0.1 microns with recentered scans and common custom segmentation): Patel NB, Wheat JL, Rodriguez A, Tran V, Harwerth RS. Agreement between retinal nerve fiber layer measures from Spectralis and Cirrus spectral domain OCT. Optom Vis Sci. 2012;89(5):E652-E666. doi:10.1097/OPX.0b013e318238c34e.
  • All other content reflects standard teaching in the core texts below; verify specifics against them.
