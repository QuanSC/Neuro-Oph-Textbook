Where the numbers come from

  • In 109 patients aged 50 or older with acute isolated CN III, IV or VI palsy, MRI and workup found a cause other than presumed microvascular ischemia in 16.5%; 10% among those with vascular risk factors only; 4.7% for isolated CN IV and VI palsies after excluding CN III palsy and GCA: Tamhankar MA, Biousse V, Ying GS, et al. Isolated third, fourth, and sixth cranial nerve palsies from presumed microvascular versus other causes: a prospective study. Ophthalmology. 2013;120(11):2264-2269.
  • Sagging eye syndrome: age-related connective tissue involution (lateral rectus to superior rectus band) causing small-angle esotropia and hypertropia in older adults: Chaudhuri Z, Demer JL. Sagging eye syndrome: connective tissue involution as a cause of horizontal and vertical strabismus in older patients. JAMA Ophthalmol. 2013;131(5):619-625. doi:10.1001/jamaophthalmol.2013.783.
  • Only 36% of acute third nerve palsies received timely arterial imaging: Chung JE, et al. J Neuroophthalmol. 2021. doi:10.1097/WNO.0000000000001337.
