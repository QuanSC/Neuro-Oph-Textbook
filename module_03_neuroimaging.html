Memory hook: on T2, water is white ("World War Two"). FLAIR is a T2 that turns CSF black, so lesions touching the ventricles pop. Fat suppression is what makes enhancement visible in the orbit, where fat is everywhere.

Where the numbers come from

  • 38.1% of pre-referral neuroimaging studies were suboptimal; leading reasons were incomplete area imaged (34.4%), wrong study type (28.1%) and poor image quality (21.9%); 28.6% of patients needed more imaging; the intraorbital optic nerve was the most common site of discordant reads: McClelland C, Van Stavern GP, Shepherd JB, Gordon M, Huecker J. Neuroimaging in patients referred to a neuro-ophthalmology service: the rates of appropriateness and concordance in interpretation. Ophthalmology. 2012;119(8):1701-1704. doi:10.1016/j.ophtha.2012.01.044.
  • Only 36% of patients with acute third nerve palsy received timely arterial imaging: Chung JE, Schroeder RM, Wilson B, Van Stavern GP, Stunkel L. Failure to obtain urgent arterial imaging in acute third nerve palsies. J Neuroophthalmol. 2021. doi:10.1097/WNO.0000000000001337.
  • Noninvasive vascular studies can miss PComm aneurysms: Elmalem VI, Hudgins PA, Bruce BB, Newman NJ, Biousse V. Underdiagnosis of posterior communicating artery aneurysm in noninvasive brain vascular studies. J Neuroophthalmol. 2011;31(2):103-109. doi:10.1097/WNO.0b013e3181f8d985.
  • Tissue brightness in the explorer is a simplified teaching model; real signal varies with scanner, technique and lesion age.
