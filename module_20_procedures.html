Biopsy or ultrasound?
A 2024 Cochrane review found temporal artery ultrasound moderately sensitive when specificity is good, but couldn't conclude whether it can replace biopsy because evidence was heterogeneous and of very low certainty. Many centers use ultrasound first where expertise exists and biopsy when the diagnosis remains uncertain.
Timing
Start steroids first when GCA is suspected; biopsy remains informative for days to weeks after treatment begins.

Where the numbers come from

  • Temporal artery ultrasound vs biopsy: Pouncey AL, Yeldham G, Magan T, Lucenteforte E, Jaffer U, Virgili G. Halo sign on temporal artery ultrasound versus temporal artery biopsy for giant cell arteritis. Cochrane Database Syst Rev. 2024;2:CD013199. In 11 paired studies, at 0.95 specificity the sensitivity of ultrasound was 0.50 vs 0.80 for biopsy; certainty very low, partly because biopsy is itself part of the ACR 1990 reference standard.
  • Temporal artery biopsy technique: Liu GT, Volpe NJ, Galetta SL. Neuro-Ophthalmology: Diagnosis and Management. 2nd ed. 2010, figure 5-52 (locate artery by palpation or Doppler; dissect to the superficial temporal fascia; ligate both ends; excise the segment).
  • Visual field recovery after pituitary surgery occurs in stages: Kerrison JB, Lynn MJ, Baer CA, et al. Stages of improvement in visual fields after pituitary tumor resection. Am J Ophthalmol. 2000;130(6):813-820.
  • Other content reflects standard teaching and the curriculum readings; follow institutional protocols for technique and consent.
