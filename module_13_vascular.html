Where the numbers come from

  • CRAO is a form of acute ischemic stroke; fewer than 20% regain functional acuity; about 95% nonarteritic and 5% arteritic; conservative treatments (ocular massage, paracentesis, hemodilution) unproven and possibly harmful: Mac Grory B, Schrag M, Biousse V, et al. Management of central retinal artery occlusion: a scientific statement from the American Heart Association. Stroke. 2021;52:e282-e294. doi:10.1161/STR.0000000000000366. Note: the AHA's requested citation gives pages e282-e294; please check the page numbers listed in the curriculum outline.
  • THEIA: IV alteplase vs aspirin within 4.5 hours (70 patients); improvement of at least 0.3 logMAR in 66% vs 48%, not statistically significant; likely underpowered; no symptomatic hemorrhage: Préterre C, Gaultier A, Obadia M, et al. Lancet Neurol. 2025;24(11):909-919. doi:10.1016/S1474-4422(25)00308-4.
  • TenCRAOS: IV tenecteplase vs aspirin within 4.5 hours (78 patients); vision recovery at 30 days in 20% vs 24% (P = 0.69); more adverse events with tenecteplase, including one fatal intracranial hemorrhage; authors do not support routine thrombolysis: Ryan SJ, Jørstad ØK, Skjelland M, et al. A randomized trial of tenecteplase in acute central retinal artery occlusion. N Engl J Med. 2026;394(5):442-450. doi:10.1056/NEJMoa2508515.
  • Ongoing: REVISION (IV alteplase vs placebo, NCT04965038) and a planned individual participant data meta-analysis (Mac Grory et al. PLoS One. 2026;21(3):e0342739). Check for updates; this evidence is changing quickly.
  • Other content reflects standard teaching in the core texts and curriculum readings.
