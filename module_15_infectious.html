Neuro-ophthalmic angle
Papilledema and CN VI palsy are common with raised pressure in cryptococcal, tuberculous and bacterial meningitis. Basal meningitis (TB, fungal, sarcoid, carcinomatous) causes multiple cranial neuropathies. Always measure opening pressure with the patient lateral decubitus.

Where the numbers come from

  • Ocular syphilis: treat like neurosyphilis even with normal CSF; aqueous crystalline penicillin G 18 to 24 million units per day IV for 10 to 14 days; CSF exam unnecessary before treatment with isolated ocular findings and no cranial nerve or neurologic signs; test for HIV: Centers for Disease Control and Prevention. Sexually Transmitted Infections Treatment Guidelines, 2021: Neurosyphilis, Ocular Syphilis, and Otosyphilis (cdc.gov); Workowski KA, Bachmann LH, Chan PA, et al. MMWR Recomm Rep. 2021;70(4):1-187.
  • ZEDS: 1 year of valacyclovir 1000 mg daily vs placebo in HZO with recent keratitis or iritis; primary 12-month endpoint not significant; secondary 18-month endpoint and multiple-episode outcomes favored valacyclovir; authors support considering 1 year of suppression: Cohen EJ, Troxel AB, Liu M, et al; ZEDS Trial Research Group. Low-dose valacyclovir in herpes zoster ophthalmicus: the Zoster Eye Disease randomized clinical trial. JAMA Ophthalmol. 2025;143(4):269-276. doi:10.1001/jamaophthalmol.2024.6114.
  • Chandler staging of orbital complications of sinusitis: Chandler JR, Langenbrunner DJ, Stevens ER. The pathogenesis of orbital complications in acute sinusitis. Laryngoscope. 1970;80(9):1414-1428. doi:10.1288/00005537-197009000-00007. Group I in the original scheme is inflammatory (preseptal) edema; later modifications (for example, the Groote Schuur classification) refine it.
