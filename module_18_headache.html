Where the numbers come from

  • Migraine with aura criteria (at least two attacks; at least three of six aura characteristics); visual aura in over 90% of migraine with aura; secondary mimics include carotid dissection, AVM and seizure: Headache Classification Committee of the International Headache Society. The International Classification of Headache Disorders, 3rd edition. Cephalalgia. 2018;38(1):1-211 (ichd-3.org).
  • Cluster headache: strictly unilateral orbital, supraorbital or temporal pain lasting 15 to 180 minutes, from every other day to 8 times a day, with ipsilateral autonomic features or restlessness; paroxysmal hemicrania 2 to 30 minutes: ICHD-3, sections 3.1 and 3.2.
  • Red flags (15 items: systemic symptoms, neoplasm, neurologic deficit, sudden onset, older age with onset after 65, pattern change, positional, Valsalva-precipitated, papilledema, progressive or atypical, pregnancy, painful eye with autonomic features, post-traumatic, immune pathology, painkiller overuse or new drug): Do TP, Remmers A, Schytz HW, et al. Red and orange flags for secondary headaches in clinical practice: SNNOOP10 list. Neurology. 2019;92(3):134-144. doi:10.1212/WNL.0000000000006697. The checklist here adapts the list for eye clinics (for example, age 50 for GCA).
  • Durations for SUNCT/SUNA (seconds to minutes) and hemicrania continua are shown schematically; see ICHD-3 for full criteria.
