Eye clinic's role
Lisch nodules and choroidal abnormalities are diagnostic criteria, and the eye exam screens for optic pathway glioma. Choroidal abnormalities can appear before Lisch nodules, which helps in young children with café-au-lait spots only.
Legius syndrome
SPRED1 variants cause café-au-lait spots and freckling without Lisch nodules, neurofibromas or gliomas. With pigmentary findings alone, NF1 is most likely, but genetic testing can distinguish the two.

Genetic testing principles
Test when a result changes diagnosis, management, surveillance or family counseling. Choose targeted testing for a known familial variant, a gene panel for a defined phenotype (such as optic atrophy or infantile nystagmus), and exome or genome sequencing for complex presentations. Variants of uncertain significance are not diagnoses. Mitochondrial DNA testing must specify mtDNA; heteroplasmy and tissue differences can produce negative blood results in some mitochondrial disorders. Pair testing with genetic counseling before and after.

Where the numbers come from

  • NF1 criteria (two or more without an affected parent; Lisch nodules or choroidal abnormalities; pathogenic NF1 variant; Legius syndrome distinction): Legius E, Messiaen L, Wolkenstein P, et al. Revised diagnostic criteria for neurofibromatosis type 1 and Legius syndrome: an international consensus recommendation. Genet Med. 2021;23(8):1506-1513. doi:10.1038/s41436-021-01170-5.
  • ROP screening: all infants with birth weight of 1500 g or less or gestational age of 30 weeks or less, plus selected at-risk infants 1500 to 2000 g or over 30 weeks; follow-up 4 to 6 months after NICU discharge for strabismus, amblyopia, refractive error, cataract and glaucoma: Fierson WM; AAP Section on Ophthalmology, AAO, AAPOS, AACO. Screening examination of premature infants for retinopathy of prematurity. Pediatrics. 2018;142(6):e20183061.
  • Optic pathway gliomas occur in 15% to 20% of individuals with NF1, mostly children under 7; many stay indolent, others cause vision loss, proptosis or precocious puberty: Listernick R, Ferner RE, Liu GT, Gutmann DH. Optic pathway gliomas in neurofibromatosis-1: controversies and recommendations. Ann Neurol. 2007;61(3):189-198. doi:10.1002/ana.21107.
  • Surveillance: annual assessments up to age 10 and at least every 2 years after; children without a known OPG examined by an experienced pediatric ophthalmologist or neuro-ophthalmologist until age 8, then annual visual screening until adulthood; routine screening MRI not indicated in well children: Carton C, Evans DG, Blanco I, et al; ERN GENTURIS NF1 Tumour Management Guideline Group. ERN GENTURIS tumour surveillance guidelines for individuals with neurofibromatosis type 1. EClinicalMedicine. 2023;56:101818. doi:10.1016/j.eclinm.2022.101818.
  • Other content reflects standard teaching in the core texts and curriculum readings.
