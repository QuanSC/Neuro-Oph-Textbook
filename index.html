How to use these modules

Keep the files together
Links work when all module files are saved in the same folder (or uploaded together to a shared drive or learning management system).
Suggested flow per module
Start with the interactive tool, work the cases before revealing answers, review the pearls, then take the self-quiz. Use the goals tab checklist to track progress.
For faculty
Each module's sources tab lists where every number came from, so content can be updated as evidence changes.
How to cite
Quan SC. Neuro-Ophthalmology: An Interactive Textbook. Version 1.0. 2026. Available at: https://quansc.github.io/Neuro-Oph-Textbook/.
Device support
Built for phones and desktops, with light and dark mode. Use the Text buttons to resize and Print or PDF for a handout.
Sharing a section
Each tab has its own link. Open a module, tap a tab, then copy the address (for example, a module's Self-quiz) to send residents straight there.
Progress
Quiz scores and goal checklists are saved in your browser only; nothing is sent anywhere. Clearing browser data or switching devices resets them.

Evidence that's changing

  • CRAO thrombolysis: THEIA (2025) and TenCRAOS (2026) showed no benefit; REVISION and an individual participant data meta-analysis are pending (Module 13).
  • GLP-1 receptor agonists: NAION safety signal (EMA 2025; NANOS and AAO 2026) and emerging use in IIH (Modules 4 and 5).
  • GCA steroid-sparing therapy: upadacitinib (SELECT-GCA 2025; FDA approval 2025) joins tocilizumab; the 2021 ACR guideline predates it (Module 4).
  • Hydroxychloroquine screening: AAO 2025 revision (published 2026) shifts primary screening to OCT and wide-pattern autofluorescence; correspondence is debating blood-level monitoring (Module 17).

About the author

Steven C. Quan, OD, FAAO, is Assistant Professor of Ophthalmology and Visual Science at the University of Chicago, where he specializes in hospital-based eye care, neuro-ophthalmology and systemic co-management of ocular disease. He studied neuroscience at UCLA, earned his Doctor of Optometry degree from the Illinois College of Optometry, and completed a residency in ocular disease at Bascom Palmer Eye Institute.

Dr. Quan leads a neuro-ophthalmology consult service and a dedicated idiopathic intracranial hypertension clinic. He directs three optometry training programs: a student externship, a one-year residency in ocular disease, and a two-year neuro-ophthalmic disease residency in partnership with the Illinois College of Optometry. His teaching emphasizes structured curricula, competency-based assessment and interprofessional collaboration, and this textbook grew out of the neuro-ophthalmology curriculum he built for his residents.

A Fellow of the American Academy of Optometry, he is active in its leadership on education and mentorship. His academic interests include optic neuropathies, ocular toxicity from cancer therapies, and the effects of social determinants of health on access and outcomes in eye care.
