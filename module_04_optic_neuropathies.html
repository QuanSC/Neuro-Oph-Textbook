Where the numbers come from

  • 15-year MS risk after optic neuritis: 50% overall; 25% with a normal baseline brain MRI vs 72% with one or more lesions; low risk without lesions in men, with disc swelling, or with atypical features: Optic Neuritis Study Group. Multiple sclerosis risk after optic neuritis: final Optic Neuritis Treatment Trial follow-up. Arch Neurol. 2008;65(6):727-732. doi:10.1001/archneur.65.6.727.
  • IV methylprednisolone speeds recovery without changing final acuity; oral prednisone alone was associated with more recurrences: Beck RW, Cleary PA, Anderson MM Jr, et al. A randomized, controlled trial of corticosteroids in the treatment of acute optic neuritis. N Engl J Med. 1992;326(9):581-588. doi:10.1056/NEJM199202273260901. (457 patients; relative risk of new episodes with oral prednisone alone 1.79.)
  • MOGAD criteria (core demyelinating event, positive MOG-IgG by cell-based assay, exclusion of better diagnoses; supporting features needed for low or untitered results): Banwell B, Bennett JL, Marignier R, et al. Diagnosis of myelin oligodendrocyte glycoprotein antibody-associated disease: International MOGAD Panel proposed criteria. Lancet Neurol. 2023;22(3):268-282. doi:10.1016/S1474-4422(22)00431-8.
  • NMOSD criteria: Wingerchuk DM, Banwell B, Bennett JL, et al. International consensus diagnostic criteria for neuromyelitis optica spectrum disorders. Neurology. 2015;85(2):177-189.
  • Tocilizumab in GCA: sustained glucocorticoid-free remission at 52 weeks in 56% (weekly) and 53% (every other week) vs 14% (placebo, 26-week taper) and 18% (placebo, 52-week taper): Stone JH, Tuckwell K, Dimonaco S, et al. Trial of tocilizumab in giant-cell arteritis. N Engl J Med. 2017;377(4):317-328. doi:10.1056/NEJMoa1613849. (Trial personnel were blinded to CRP because tocilizumab normalizes it.)
  • Upadacitinib in GCA: sustained remission at week 52 in 46.4% (15 mg daily plus 26-week steroid taper) vs 29.0% (placebo plus 52-week taper); 7.5 mg was not superior; 428 patients, 70% new-onset: Blockmans D, Penn SK, Setty AR, et al. A phase 3 trial of upadacitinib for giant-cell arteritis. N Engl J Med. 2025;392(20):2013-2024. doi:10.1056/NEJMoa2413449. (FDA approval for GCA, April 2025; the 2021 ACR guideline predates it.)
  • GCA management (ACR/VF): temporal artery biopsy preferred over ultrasound or MRI for diagnosis in the US, long segment over 1 cm, ideally within 2 weeks of starting steroids; pulse IV steroids for threatened vision loss; tocilizumab with oral steroids over steroids alone for new GCA; baseline large-vessel imaging; aspirin only for critical or flow-limiting carotid or vertebral involvement; observation, not escalation, for rising inflammatory markers alone; long-term monitoring in remission (the only strong GCA recommendation; nearly all others conditional): Maz M, Chung SA, Abril A, et al. 2021 American College of Rheumatology/Vasculitis Foundation guideline for the management of giant cell arteritis and Takayasu arteritis. Arthritis Rheumatol. 2021;73(8):1349-1365. doi:10.1002/art.41774.
  • Treat-to-target in GCA: the target is remission (no clinical symptoms and no systemic inflammation), plus prevention of ischemia and vascular damage; monitor every 1 to 4 weeks until remission, then every 3 to 6 months in stable remission (expert opinion): Dejaco C, Kerschbaumer A, Aletaha D, et al. Treat-to-target recommendations in giant cell arteritis and polymyalgia rheumatica. Ann Rheum Dis. 2024;83(1):48-57. doi:10.1136/ard-2022-223429.
  • Semaglutide and NAION: EMA safety committee classed NAION as a very rare side effect (up to 1 in 10,000 users), with about a two-fold risk in type 2 diabetes in epidemiological studies; stop semaglutide if NAION is confirmed: European Medicines Agency, PRAC conclusion, June 2025 (ema.europa.eu). See also the NANOS and AAO clinical statement on GLP-1 receptor agonists and NAION (aao.org, 2026), which recommends shared decision-making.
  • Optic nerve decompression is not effective for NAION and may be harmful: Ischemic Optic Neuropathy Decompression Trial Research Group. Optic nerve decompression surgery for nonarteritic anterior ischemic optic neuropathy (NAION) is not effective and may be harmful. JAMA. 1995;273(8):625-632. doi:10.1001/jama.273.8.625.
  • Other content reflects standard teaching in the core texts; verify specifics against them.
