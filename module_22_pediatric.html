Pediatric topics

How children differ from adults

History comes from others
Parents notice behavior (bumping into things, head tilts, eye rubbing, sitting close to the TV), not symptoms. Young children rarely complain of vision loss, even when it's severe in one eye.
Exam is adapted
Fixation and following, preferential looking (Teller cards), optotypes matched to age, cover tests with toys, quick confrontation fields with targets; OCT and ERG under sedation when needed.
Disease spectrum differs
Optic neuritis in children is more often bilateral with disc swelling and MOG antibodies, and may follow infection or ADEM. Tumors (optic pathway glioma, craniopharyngioma, medulloblastoma) and hydrocephalus are leading causes of optic atrophy and papilledema.
Raised pressure looks different
Infants with open sutures may not develop papilledema; pediatric IIH uses a higher opening pressure threshold (280 mm CSF, or 250 if not sedated and not obese).
The visual system is still developing
Unequal input causes amblyopia; strabismus and ptosis can be vision-threatening; early treatment matters.
Nystagmus and abnormal eye movements are clues
Infantile nystagmus often points to an afferent disorder; acquired nystagmus, monocular nystagmus or opsoclonus points to the brain or a tumor.
