Rule: bilateral disc swelling with normal visual function is papilledema until proven otherwise. Image the brain with venography the same day if symptoms or exam suggest raised pressure.

Secondary causes to exclude: venous sinus thrombosis or obstruction, medications (tetracyclines, vitamin A and retinoids, lithium, corticosteroid withdrawal, growth hormone), sleep apnea, anemia, renal failure, endocrine disorders (Addison disease, hypoparathyroidism). Typical IIH patients are women of childbearing age with obesity or recent weight gain; men and patients without obesity need an especially careful search.

Treatment ladder

  1. Weight loss for patients with obesity. In IIH:WT, bariatric surgery lowered intracranial pressure more than a community weight program at 12 and 24 months (Mollan 2021).
  2. Acetazolamide, titrated to the maximum tolerated dose (up to 4 g/day in the IIHTT). Added to diet, it modestly improved visual fields and papilledema in mild vision loss (Wall 2014).
  3. Headache management: topiramate is a common alternative or add-on, with weight loss as a bonus. Watch for medication-overuse headache.
  4. Surgery for fulminant or progressive vision loss: CSF shunting, optic nerve sheath fenestration, or venous sinus stenting in selected patients with stenosis and a pressure gradient.
  5. Emerging: GLP-1 receptor agonists are under study for IIH. They are not yet standard treatment for IIH itself, and their NAION signal (Module 4) deserves discussion.
Higher-risk features for poor visual outcome (IIHTT)
Male sex, high-grade papilledema, low baseline acuity and frequent transient visual obscurations. Monitor these patients more closely and escalate sooner (Wall 2015).
Fulminant IIH
Rapid vision loss over days to weeks needs admission and urgent surgical CSF diversion; medical therapy alone is not enough.

Where the numbers come from

  • Diagnostic criteria, opening pressure thresholds, and the without-papilledema pathways: Friedman DI, Liu GT, Digre KB. Revised diagnostic criteria for the pseudotumor cerebri syndrome in adults and children. Neurology. 2013;81(13):1159-1165. doi:10.1212/WNL.0b013e3182a55f17.
  • IIHTT: in mild vision loss (PMD -2 to -7 dB), acetazolamide up to 4 g/day plus diet improved PMD by 1.43 dB vs 0.71 dB with placebo plus diet (difference 0.71 dB, P = .050) and improved papilledema grade: NORDIC IIH Study Group Writing Committee; Wall M, McDermott MP, Kieburtz KD, et al. JAMA. 2014;311(16):1641-1651. doi:10.1001/jama.2014.3312.
  • Risk factors for poor visual outcome in the IIHTT: Wall M, Falardeau J, Fletcher WA, et al. Risk factors for poor visual outcome in patients with idiopathic intracranial hypertension. Neurology. 2015;85(9):799-805.
  • IIH:WT: bariatric surgery superior to community weight management for lowering intracranial pressure: Mollan SP, Mitchell JL, Ottridge RS, et al. JAMA Neurol. 2021;78(6):678-686. doi:10.1001/jamaneurol.2021.0659.
  • Frisén staging scheme: Frisén L. Swelling of the optic nerve head: a staging scheme. J Neurol Neurosurg Psychiatry. 1982;45(1):13-18. doi:10.1136/jnnp.45.1.13. The original stages are ordinal with unequal steps, so they shouldn't be averaged (Frisén L. J Neuroophthalmol. 2017;37(1):3-6).
