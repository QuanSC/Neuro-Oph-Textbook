Visual field

Tap a red numbered site. Sites run front to back: optic nerve, chiasm, tract, LGN, radiations, cortex.

Rule of thumb: anything before the chiasm is one eye; the chiasm is both temporal fields; everything after is homonymous. Posterior lesions tend to be more congruous, but congruity is a weak localizer on its own: in a series of 904 homonymous hemianopias, nearly every defect type occurred at every retrochiasmal location (Zhang 2006). Use pupils, discs and imaging too.

Where the numbers come from

  • Congruity as a localizer: Zhang X, Kedar S, Lynn MJ, Newman NJ, Biousse V. Homonymous hemianopias: clinical-anatomic correlations in 904 cases. Neurology. 2006;66(6):906-910. Every type of homonymous defect except temporal crescent loss and sectoranopia occurred at all retrochiasmal locations.
  • CN II is about 50 mm long: intraocular about 1 mm, intraorbital about 25 mm, intracanalicular about 9 mm, intracranial about 16 mm (canalicular and intracranial lengths vary between sources): StatPearls, Compressive Optic Neuropathy (NCBI Bookshelf NBK560583, updated 2025). About 53% crossing at the chiasm is standard teaching (BCSC Section 5).
  • Wilbrand's knee: presented as debated, because its existence has been questioned in primate studies; the junctional scotoma pattern itself is clinically reliable.
  • LGN sectoranopias: horizontal wedge from lateral (posterior) choroidal artery ischemia, and quadruple sectoranopia (upper and lower sectors lost, horizontal zone spared) from distal anterior choroidal artery ischemia. Frisén L, Holmegaard L, Rosencrantz M. J Neurol Neurosurg Psychiatry. 1978;41(4):374-380; Frisén L. J Neurol Neurosurg Psychiatry. 1979;42(7):590-594. Rarely, horizontal sectoranopia comes from optic radiation or occipital lesions.
